Rising Tide — Morning Scan

As of October 23, 2026. "Picks and shovels" = companies that sell the tools to the big AI/space players, like selling shovels in a gold rush. Figures come from web search summaries (some from blogs) — verify before investing. Not financial advice.

1. NEW CANDIDATES

"Established" = already makes real money. "Moonshot" = newer, riskier, may not be profitable yet.

  • Aixtron (AIXA.DE) — Frankfurt, Germany | Semiconductors | Established. Makes machines that grow thin crystal layers used in power chips and lasers for data-center networking. Its preliminary Q3 2026 orders and profit beat what analysts expected (guidance for the year unchanged). More chips and optical links in AI data centers means more machine orders.
  • ASM International (ASM.AS) — Amsterdam, Netherlands | Chip equipment | Established. Sells tools that deposit atom-thin layers onto chips. Reported a record Q2 2026. Chipmakers building AI chips must buy this kind of tool. (Its US-traded form is an ADR — a US-listed receipt for a foreign share — but I have not confirmed that ticker.)
  • Harmonic Drive Systems (6324.T) — Tokyo, Japan | Robotics | Established. Makes the compact precision gears inside robot joints. It said humanoid-robot orders were rising (late-2025 figures; I found no fresh October data). Risks: pricey shares, reliance on one big car-industry customer, and cheaper Chinese rivals.
  • Modine Manufacturing (MOD) — NYSE | Data-center cooling | Established. Reportedly has over $4 billion of cooling capacity reserved by big data-center builders through 2029. Hotter AI chips need more cooling.
  • Quanta Services (PWR) — NYSE | Power grid construction | Established. Builds power lines and substations. Reported record backlog (signed future work) of $53.4 billion in Q2 2026. Data centers need huge new power connections.
  • Eaton (ETN) — NYSE | Electrical equipment | Established. Said AI data centers are its biggest end market this year, about 21% of sales.
  • USA Rare Earth (USAR) — NASDAQ | Rare earth magnets | Moonshot. In June 2026 the Commerce Department finalized up to $277 million in incentives plus a loan of up to $1.3 billion; in return the government gets shares and warrants (rights to buy shares later). Magnets go into robots, drones and EV motors.
  • Pensana (PRE.L) — London | Rare earths | Moonshot. Building its Longonjo rare earth mine in Angola (about 25% built, first output targeted 2028). Said to be in talks on US support for non-Chinese supply. Ticker should be double-checked.

2. UPCOMING IPOs

IPO = a private company selling shares to the public for the first time. Rumored = only press reports. Confidentially filed = paperwork sent privately to the SEC (US regulator). S-1 filing = the public version of that paperwork. Priced = the share price is set and trading starts. Lockup date = the day insiders are first allowed to sell their shares after an IPO, which can push the price down.

  • Westinghouse (nuclear reactor maker; private, no ticker yet) — reportedly confidentially filed July 31, 2026; one report says a public filing could come as early as October. Single-source, unconfirmed.
  • Fervo Energy (FRVO) — geothermal power. In May 2026 it raised its price range to $25–26 and upsized to 70 million shares. I could not confirm final pricing; status unverified.
  • Cerebras Systems (CBRS) — AI chips. Priced May 2026 at $185 per share; jumped on day one. Lockup date not found (typically about 180 days, i.e. roughly mid-November — an estimate, not confirmed).
  • X-Energy (XE) — small nuclear reactors. Priced April 2026. Correction to my June note: the company's SEC filing says the lockup ends early on September 1, 2026 (not Oct 21), so it has already passed.
  • Nscale (NSCL) — AI data-center builder, London. Sources conflict (mid-October date vs. S-1 filed). Status unclear; ticker unverified.
  • Sierra Space (private, no ticker) — rumored IPO by year-end, but its CFO left, so it looks unlikely. Helsing (private, no ticker) — rumored European defense-AI IPO, no date.

3. SECTOR CATALYSTS

  • Data-center spending. Capex (money spent on buildings and equipment) estimates are huge but vary: one source says top-five cloud giants spend about $602 billion in 2026, another says $330 billion. Either way, it flows to cooling, power and chip suppliers like Vertiv Holdings (VRT), which raised its full-year outlook after Q2.
  • Grid bottleneck. Transformers and switchgear (equipment that routes electricity) have very long wait times, and hooking up to the grid can take 5–7 years. That favors makers of this gear, such as GE Vernova (GEV) and Quanta above.
  • Chip equipment boom. Industry group forecasts for 2026 equipment spending: about $143.9 billion, up roughly 23%. Applied Materials (AMAT) posted record fiscal Q3 revenue of $9.115 billion. ASML (ASML) reported around Oct 14; I did not find the actual numbers.
  • Rare earths. US government is backing non-Chinese supply (a reported $3 billion support push in August, equity-for-funding deals like USAR's). Reported China export controls due to take effect in November 2026 are a risk to watch. Helps MP Materials (MP).
