<h2>Rising Tide — Morning Scan</h2>
<p><b>As of October 18, 2026.</b> Picks and shovels: the companies that sell tools and parts to the big AI, space and tech players. Educational only, not financial advice. I did not verify prices or financials, so none are quoted. Check every ticker and number yourself before acting.</p>

<h3>1. NEW CANDIDATES</h3>
<p>"Established" = already makes real money. "Moonshot" = newer and riskier, may not be profitable yet.</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/FN">Fabrinet (FN)</a> — NYSE, Thailand-based operations. Optical components. <b>Established.</b> It builds the light-based data links (optical transceivers) that connect AI chips inside data centers. It is reported to be a key maker for Nvidia, so bigger AI clusters mean more orders for it.</li>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ONTO">Onto Innovation (ONTO)</a> — NYSE, US. Chip inspection tools. <b>Established.</b> Its machines check advanced chip packaging (how several chips are stacked together) for flaws. AI chips use lots of this packaging. An Oppenheimer analyst named it a "pick-and-shovel" company in July 2026.</li>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UCTT">Ultra Clean Holdings (UCTT)</a> — NASDAQ, US. Chip-equipment parts. <b>Established.</b> Makes gas and fluid delivery systems that go inside chip-making machines. When chipmakers buy more machines, it sells more parts. Same Oppenheimer mention.</li>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CAMT">Camtek (CAMT)</a> — NASDAQ, Israel. Chip inspection. <b>Established.</b> It does not make AI chips. It makes the tools that make sure they work. Coverage says it has hit record sales, but the stock may already be priced high.</li>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/PL">Planet Labs (PL)</a> — NYSE, US. Satellite imagery. <b>Moonshot.</b> Runs fleets of small Earth-imaging satellites and sells the pictures to governments and companies. More defense and space spending can lift it. I found no fresh coverage today, so check its profit status yourself.</li>
</ul>

<h3>2. UPCOMING IPOs</h3>
<p>Terms: <b>IPO</b> = a private company sells shares to the public for the first time. <b>Confidentially filed</b> = paperwork sent to regulators in private. <b>S-1 filed</b> = the public paperwork a company files with the SEC (the US market regulator) before going public. <b>Priced</b> = the IPO share price is set and trading starts. <b>Lockup date</b> = the day insiders are first allowed to sell their shares, usually about 180 days after the IPO. Stocks can drop when it arrives.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Nothing new in our sectors.</b> Renaissance Capital's calendar (a firm that tracks IPOs) shows no IPOs set to price this week. Its newest filings are two SPACs (empty "blank check" shell companies that raise cash to buy a business later) and a biotech, Aspen Neuroscience. Biotechs City Therapeutics and Iambic Therapeutics priced last week, but they are drug developers, not lab-tool makers.</li>
<li><b>Update on last digest:</b> <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CBRS">Cerebras Systems (CBRS)</a> (AI chips) is no longer waiting. Sources say it <b>priced</b> on May 13, 2026 at $185 per share, raising about $5.55 billion. Its lockup date is reported as around mid-November 2026 (one source only, so verify).</li>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XE">X-Energy (XE)</a> (small nuclear reactors): <b>priced</b> in April 2026. Our earlier estimate put its lockup date near October 21, 2026. I could not re-confirm it, so check the exact date.</li>
<li>Anthropic (not in our sectors, and no ticker exists) is reported to be possibly filing soon, with a listing possibly in mid-November. It could pull investor attention and cash away from smaller IPOs.</li>
</ul>

<h3>3. SECTOR CATALYSTS</h3>
<ul>
<li><b>Big Tech AI spending (capex = money spent on buildings and equipment).</b> Sources put the biggest cloud companies' combined 2026 capex at roughly $700 billion or more. Estimates differ by source and some are from blogs. Third-quarter earnings reports are not out yet and should arrive in the coming weeks. They are the next check on whether spending keeps rising. That money flows to chip tools, cooling, power and optical suppliers like the ones above.</li>
<li><b>Chip equipment demand.</b> Applied Materials reportedly said its equipment business will grow more than 30% in 2026, up from an earlier 20% forecast. That helps toolmakers broadly, including <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ONTO">Onto Innovation (ONTO)</a> and <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UCTT">Ultra Clean Holdings (UCTT)</a>. (<a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMAT">Applied Materials (AMAT)</a> is a large company, so it is context here, not a pick.)</li>
<li><b>Rare earth squeeze.</b> China tightened export licenses in October 2025 for rare earths (metals used in magnets and chip tools) linked to advanced chips. A TrendForce report from August 2026 says Japan's imports of dysprosium (a key magnet metal) fell 82% in the first half of 2026 versus 2024. Shortages like this help non-China suppliers such as <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MP">MP Materials (MP)</a>.</li>
<li><b>Nuclear power for data centers.</b> Crusoe and Aalo Atomics announced a deal on July 30, 2026 to power a small data center with nuclear at Idaho National Laboratory, targeted for 2027. It is early-stage, but it shows data-center firms want their own power. Related names: <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/OKLO">Oklo (OKLO)</a> and <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BWXT">BWX Technologies (BWXT)</a>.</li>
<li><b>Memory costs.</b> Microsoft reportedly blamed rising memory chip prices for part of its higher spending plans. Only three firms dominate DRAM (the main memory chip), so prices can swing a lot.</li>
</ul>
