# Rising Tide — Morning Scan
**As of September 28, 2026** | Picks & shovels across AI, space, and technology infrastructure

This digest covers **upstream opportunities** — the suppliers, toolmakers, and infrastructure companies that giant AI, space, and tech players depend on. Think of it like a gold rush: instead of buying the mining company everyone is talking about, we look at who makes the pickaxes and shovels those miners need. When the big players grow, these companies grow with them.

## Section 2: Upcoming IPOs

*An **IPO** (Initial Public Offering) is when a private company sells shares to the public for the first time. A company usually goes through stages: **rumored** (no filing yet, just reporting) → **confidentially filed** (papers submitted privately to regulators, not yet public) → **S-1 filed** (the public paperwork is out) → **priced** (shares are sold and trading begins). A **lockup date** is when early investors and employees are first allowed to sell their shares after an IPO — usually about 180 days later — and it can pressure the stock price when it hits.*

## Section 3: Sector Catalysts

*Big-picture events, policy changes, or spending announcements that can lift an entire sector — not just one company.*

**🔹 Money Is Rotating From Chipmakers Into Chip-Equipment Makers**

In late September 2026, investors began pulling money out of Nvidia-heavy AI funds and into funds that own the whole chip supply chain — the equipment makers behind the chips, not just the chip designers. JPMorgan projects industry-wide spending on wafer fabrication equipment (WFE — the machines used to physically build chips) will jump 31% in 2026 and 38% more in 2027. Lam Research raised its own 2026 forecast for that spending to $140 billion. Benefits [Lasertec (LSRCY)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/LSRCY) and [Advantest (ADTTF)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ADTTF) above.

**🔹 Big Tech's Nuclear Buying Spree**

Microsoft, Amazon, Google, and Meta have together signed more nuclear power purchase agreements (offtake deals — long-term contracts to buy a plant's future power output before it's even built) in the past eighteen months than the US signed in the previous twenty years. A single AI data-center campus under construction today can draw 1–2 gigawatts of power continuously — enough for a mid-sized city. This is the demand driver behind [X-Energy (XE)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/XE)'s upcoming lockup and Westinghouse's IPO plans above.

**🔹 The Transformer Shortage Isn't Going Away**

The US faces an estimated 30% shortage in large power transformers and a 10% shortage in smaller distribution transformers, with lead times stretching to four years in some cases and prices up more than 70% since 2019. About 80% of US transformers are imported. This is filling the order books of [Hubbell (HUBB)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/HUBB) and [GE Vernova (GEV)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GEV) above for years to come.

**🔹 Data-Center Cooling Is Consolidating Fast**

In the past year, Eaton bought cooling-hardware maker Boyd Thermal for $9.5 billion, Ecolab bought CoolIT for $4.75 billion, Trane bought LiquidStack, and Schneider Electric folded in Motivair. Big industrial companies are racing to buy their way into liquid cooling rather than build it — a sign of how urgently AI racks need it, and a reminder that independent cooling specialists can become acquisition targets almost overnight.

**🔹 Hyperscaler Spending Keeps Climbing**

Google just raised its 2026 capex (capital expenditures — spending on physical assets like buildings and equipment) guidance to $195–205 billion, up from $180–190 billion just one quarter earlier. Across the 14 largest data-center operators globally, 2026 capex is tracking toward roughly $750 billion, up from about $450 billion last year. Nearly every dollar of that eventually flows upstream to the chip, power, and cooling suppliers in Section 1.

**🔹 China Tightens the Rare Earth Magnet Tap Again**

China's exports of rare earth magnets dropped 21% in the run-up to trade talks between the US and China in late September 2026, with no new trade truce confirmed as of this writing. Every time Chinese export flows tighten, it adds urgency to the push for non-Chinese rare earth supply — the trend behind [REalloys (ALOY)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ALOY) above, alongside MP Materials and USA Rare Earth from our last digest.
