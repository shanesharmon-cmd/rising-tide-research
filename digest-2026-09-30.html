Rising Tide — Morning Scan

As of September 30, 2026. "Picks and shovels" means companies that sell the tools and parts to the big AI, space and tech players. When the giants grow, these suppliers can grow too. Not financial advice. Figures come from news search results; verify before acting.

1. NEW CANDIDATES

Astera Labs (ALAB) — Nasdaq, US | Semiconductors | Established
Makes small chips that move data between processors inside AI data centers. Reported Q1 2026 revenue of $308.4 million, up 93% from a year earlier. It also has a warrant deal with Amazon worth $6.5 billion (a warrant is a right to buy shares later at a set price).

Credo Technology (CRDO) — Nasdaq, US | Semiconductors / data-center cabling | Established
Sells special cables that link server racks over short distances, a cheaper option than fiber-optic links. Reported fiscal Q2 2026 revenue of $268 million, up 272%. More AI servers means more cables.

Onto Innovation (ONTO) — Nasdaq, US | Chip-making equipment | Established
Makes inspection tools that check chips for defects during manufacturing. Named in AI supply-chain coverage. I did not find fresh financial figures, so check its latest results yourself.

Aixtron (AIXA.DE) — Frankfurt, Germany | Chip-making equipment | Established
Builds machines that grow the thin crystal layers used in lasers and power chips. Coverage says its laser-chip business, tied to AI data-center light links, could double its revenue contribution in 2026. It trades in Germany; I am not sure of a US ADR (a US-listed share of a foreign company).

Lynas Rare Earths (LYC.AX) — ASX, Australia | Rare earths | Established
One of the few big non-Chinese rare earth producers (metals used in magnets for motors, robots and missiles). A July 2026 US directive said defense contractors must avoid Chinese raw materials, which points demand to suppliers like this. I did not verify Lynas's financials.

2. UPCOMING IPOs

An IPO is when a private company first sells shares to the public. An S-1 filing is the paperwork a company files with regulators before going public. A lockup date is when insiders, barred from selling for about 180 days after an IPO, are allowed to sell. That can push a stock down.

3. SECTOR CATALYSTS

Chip equipment spending forecast raised. Morgan Stanley raised its 2026 forecast for spending on chip-factory equipment from $122 billion to $128 billion (10% growth). That helps toolmakers such as Applied Materials (AMAT) and Lam Research (LRCX). A new US export rule limiting some tool sales to China is a risk to the same group.

Rare earth supply squeeze. China's export pause is set to expire November 10. In June it put MP Materials (MP) and USA Rare Earth (USAR) on an export control list. It cuts them off from Chinese-origin dual-use items (goods with civilian and military uses). The US July rule pushing defense contractors away from Chinese materials points demand the other way.

Nuclear power for data centers. The IAEA (the UN nuclear agency) held a meeting Sept 28–30 on powering and cooling data centers with small reactors. Vertiv (VRT) is partnering with SGC Energy on an AI data center in South Korea (Sept 8 memorandum, a non-binding agreement).
