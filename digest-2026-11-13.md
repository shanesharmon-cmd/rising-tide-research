# Rising Tide — Morning Scan, November 13, 2026

**As of Friday, November 13, 2026 (about 10 a.m. US Eastern).** Prices and figures come from news reports this week. Check a live quote before acting on anything.

This digest looks for "picks and shovels" companies. These are the suppliers and toolmakers that the giant AI, space, and tech companies depend on. When the giants spend more, these companies often sell more.

**Quick backdrop:** Tech stocks have had a rough two weeks. The Nasdaq fell into a "correction" (a drop of 10% or more from its recent high). Many chip stocks are down sharply from their peaks. Meanwhile, the companies themselves keep reporting strong orders. That gap between strong business and weak share prices is the theme of this week.

## 2. UPCOMING IPOs

Quick terms:
- **IPO** (initial public offering): when a private company first sells shares to the public.
- **Confidentially filed**: the company has sent draft paperwork to regulators privately. The public can't see the details yet.
- **S-1 filed**: the company has publicly filed its S-1 (the paperwork a company files with regulators before it can go public).
- **Priced**: the final share price is set, and trading starts right after.
- **Lockup date**: the date when insiders (founders, early investors) are first allowed to sell their shares, which can push the price down.

## 3. SECTOR CATALYSTS

**Chip-making equipment: a big forecast from a toolmaker.**
<a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AMAT">Applied Materials (AMAT)</a>, the largest US maker of chip-factory machines, reported this week. Its chip-equipment sales grew 64% to $8.0 billion. It expects global spending on wafer fab equipment (the machines inside chip factories) to rise more than 30% in 2027. That forecast lifts the whole toolmaker group, including <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TER">Teradyne (TER)</a>. Also, <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/TSM">TSMC (TSM)</a>, the world's largest contract chipmaker, approved investments of up to $500 million each in Anthropic and OpenAI. That ties the chip factory even more closely to AI demand. *Plain-English takeaway:* the stocks are down, but the toolmakers are saying 2027 orders look strong.

**Rare earths: China delays its export limits again.**
On Nov. 9, hours before a deadline, China extended its pause on newer rare-earth export controls (limits on selling these metals abroad) to January 10, 2027. Older limits on key magnet metals like dysprosium and terbium are still in place. *Why it matters:* the threat isn't gone, it's just delayed. That keeps pressure on the US and allies to build their own supply. It helps companies like <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MP">MP Materials (MP)</a>, <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/USAR">USA Rare Earth (USAR)</a>, and <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/UUUU">Energy Fuels (UUUU)</a>. Another deadline lands around then: a Pentagon cutoff for Chinese-made magnets in US weapons.

**Nuclear power for data centers: more deals, but still early.**
X-energy's Amazon-funded Indiana project (above) is the newest example of tech giants paying for reactor planning. Separately, <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SMR">NuScale Power (SMR)</a>'s power-sale talks with the Tennessee Valley Authority are still unsigned as of Nov. 9. *Takeaway:* lots of announcements, few binding contracts so far. Moonshot territory.

**Space & defense satellites: Europe is spending.**
France awarded <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/AIR.PA">Airbus (AIR.PA)</a> and Thales Alenia Space, a joint venture of <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/HO.PA">Thales (HO.PA)</a> and <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/LDO.MI">Leonardo (LDO.MI)</a>, a contract worth more than €1.5 billion (about $1.7 billion) for two military spy satellites. A newsletter also reported the UK defense ministry favoring <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/LMT">Lockheed Martin (LMT)</a> for a £2.4 billion Skynet military-communications satellite contract (single source, unconfirmed). Smaller names getting defense work: <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BKSY">BlackSky (BKSY)</a>, a satellite-imaging company, reportedly won a seven-figure defense contract (single source). <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MDA.TO">MDA Space (MDA.TO)</a>, a Canadian satellite builder, rose about 1% after confirming tests on satellites for <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/GSAT">Globalstar (GSAT)</a>. *Takeaway:* European governments are spending more on their own space-defense systems. That's a multi-year tailwind for satellite builders and their suppliers.

**Data-center cooling & robotics / biotech lab tools:** nothing new today. (I found no fresh November contracts or results in these areas.)
