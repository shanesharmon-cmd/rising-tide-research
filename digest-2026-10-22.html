2. UPCOMING IPOs

Key terms: an S-1 filing is the paperwork a company files with regulators before it can go public. Confidentially filed means the paperwork was sent privately, so the public can't see it yet. Rumored means only press reports. Priced means the share price is set and trading is starting. A lockup date is the day early insiders are first allowed to sell their shares (usually about 6 months after the IPO), which can push the price down.

Warning: everything below comes from one April 24, 2026 news roundup. It is about six months old. I found no current IPO calendar, so check each status before acting. I could not confirm tickers for these companies, so I have not added links. No lockup dates found.

  • Cerebras Systems — AI chips. Status per April report: S-1 filed, aiming to raise about $2 billion. Ticker unconfirmed. May have already listed.
  • X-energy — small modular nuclear reactors (small, factory-built power plants). April sources disagreed; one said priced at $23 a share, raising about $1 billion. Ticker unconfirmed.
  • Fervo Energy — geothermal power (energy from underground heat). Status per April report: S-1 filed for a Nasdaq listing, about $250 million estimated. Ticker unconfirmed.
  • Altera — programmable chips. Status per April report: rumored, preparing a confidential filing. Timing unclear. Ticker unconfirmed.
  • HawkEye 360 — space-based radio signal tracking for defense. Status per April report: S-1 filed for the NYSE. Ticker unconfirmed.

3. SECTOR CATALYSTS

  • Data-center spending is enormous. One 2026 article says Big Tech will spend about $735 billion on AI data centers this year. A TrendForce headline says about $830 billion for the top nine cloud companies. The numbers differ by source, so treat them as rough. Capex (money spent on buildings and equipment) flows to the suppliers above.
  • Power and cooling are the fastest-growing part. Research firm Omdia says spending on physical infrastructure like power and cooling is growing about 18% a year.
  • Electricity is the bottleneck. One analysis says power availability, not land or chips, now decides where new data centers can go. One outlet also reports over $130 billion of projects delayed by community opposition in Q1 2026 (single source, unverified). That is a risk to the whole sector.
  • Rare earths: a Mugglehead roundup mentions a $500 million DOE (US Department of Energy) call for critical minerals funding in March 2026, and a US rare earth company seeking DOE backing in June 2026. Both are older news, and I did not confirm details.
