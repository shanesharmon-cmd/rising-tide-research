<h3>1. NEW CANDIDATES</h3>
<p><i>"Established" means already makes real money. "Moonshot" means newer and riskier, and may not be profitable yet.</i></p>
<ul>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BE">Bloom Energy (BE)</a> — NYSE, USA — power equipment. <b>Established.</b> It makes fuel cells (boxes that make electricity from gas on-site, with no wait for the grid). Search results say Oracle agreed to buy up to 2.8 gigawatts (a gigawatt is about the power of a large power plant) and that total backlog (orders not yet delivered) is about $20 billion. Data centers need power now, and Bloom can deliver it faster than the grid.</li>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/LITE">Lumentum Holdings (LITE)</a> — Nasdaq, USA — semiconductors/optics. <b>Established.</b> It makes the lasers and optical modules that move data between chips in AI data centers. Bigger AI clusters need more of them. I did not find fresh figures, so check its latest results.</li>
<li><a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/APLD">Applied Digital (APLD)</a> — Nasdaq, USA — data-center builder. <b>Moonshot.</b> It builds and leases data-center space. Sources cite about $16 billion of signed lease revenue over 15-year deals, including one with CoreWeave (a cloud company renting out AI chips). Fast growth, but it carries debt and building risk.</li>
</ul>
<p>Still worth watching from last scan: <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/VRT">Vertiv Holdings (VRT)</a> (data-center cooling and power; its backlog was reported at about $15 billion).</p>

Lockup dates below are my estimates from the standard 180 days. Verify them in each company's filings.</p>

<h3>3. SECTOR CATALYSTS</h3>
<ul>
<li><b>Power is the bottleneck.</b> Several sources say the limit on AI growth has moved from chips to electricity, cooling, and materials. That helps <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/VRT">Vertiv (VRT)</a> and <a href="https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BE">Bloom Energy (BE)</a>.</li>
<li><b>Big-tech spending (capex means money spent on buildings and equipment).</b> One source says Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft and Meta may spend roughly $720–745 billion in 2026. That money flows to suppliers.</li>
<li><b>Small nuclear reactors.</b> Reports describe about 22 gigawatts in development, with Google's deal with Kairos Power targeting a first unit by 2030. This is a long-term lift for the sector.</li>

</ul>
