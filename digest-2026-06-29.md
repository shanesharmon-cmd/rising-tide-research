# Rising Tide — Morning Scan
**As of June 29, 2026** | Picks & shovels across AI, space, and technology infrastructure

This digest covers **upstream opportunities** — the suppliers, toolmakers, materials, and infrastructure companies that giant AI, space, and tech players depend on. Think of it as the gold-rush framing: instead of buying the mining company everyone talks about, we look at who makes the pickaxes and shovels those miners need.

## Section 1: New Candidates

*Each entry is tagged **established** (already makes real money) or **moonshot** (earlier-stage and riskier, may not yet be profitable).*

### Semiconductors & Chip-Making Equipment

**[Onto Innovation (ONTO)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ONTO)** — Nasdaq, USA | Semiconductor Equipment | **Established**

Makes the specialized cameras and sensors that check whether chips on a silicon wafer (a thin disc of silicon used to make chips in bulk) are flawless before they ship. Every advanced chip factory requires wafer inspection tools — and with hyperscalers ordering record numbers of AI chips, factories are buying more. Onto posted record revenue in 2025 and guides for continued outperformance in 2026. Less covered than the big equipment names (ASML, Applied Materials), but structurally essential.

**[Axcelis Technologies (ACLS)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/ACLS)** — Nasdaq, USA | Semiconductor Equipment | **Established**

Makes ion implanters — machines that "dope" silicon wafers by firing specific atoms into them to give chips their electrical properties. No chip gets made without this step. Axcelis recently agreed to merge with [Veeco Instruments (VECO)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/VECO) in an all-stock deal, creating a stronger combined company in equipment for power chips made from silicon carbide (SiC), a material used in EV motors and data-center power converters.

### Power / Grid & Small Modular Nuclear (SMR)

**[BWX Technologies (BWXT)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/BWXT)** — NYSE, USA | Defense / Nuclear | **Established**

The sole manufacturer of nuclear reactors for US Navy submarines and aircraft carriers — no competitor can legally step in. BWXT is now expanding into SMRs (small modular reactors — factory-built nuclear reactors smaller than a city block, designed to power data centers, military bases, and towns) and components for the Golden Dome missile defense system. Posted record 2025 revenue of $3.2B with backlog up 50%. Guides $3.75B in 2026 revenue. Often overlooked because it sits at the intersection of defense and nuclear.

**[Centrus Energy (LEU)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/LEU)** — NYSE American, USA | Nuclear Fuel | **Established**

The only US company licensed to produce HALEU (high-assay low-enriched uranium — a more potent nuclear fuel grade that next-generation SMRs and micro-reactors require). Oklo Inc. just signed a letter of intent with Centrus for fuel supply starting in 2029. If advanced nuclear takes off, Centrus is the domestic gatekeeper of the fuel supply.

**[Nano Nuclear Energy (NNE)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/NNE)** — Nasdaq, USA | Micro-Reactors | **Moonshot**

Developing portable micro-reactors small enough to truck to a remote location — designed to power military forward bases, disaster zones, and off-grid AI facilities. No revenue yet; this is early-stage R&D. The concept addresses a real gap that batteries and diesel generators cannot fill. Highly speculative.

### Rare Earths & Specialty Materials

**[MP Materials (MP)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MP)** — NYSE, USA | Rare Earths | **Established**

Operates the only significant rare earth mine in the US (Mountain Pass, California) and is scaling domestic magnet manufacturing. China controls ~90% of global rare earth refining and has escalated export restrictions in 2026 — making MP the most liquid (easy to buy and sell) US-listed vehicle for Western rare earth exposure. AI data centers, EV motors, and defense systems all require rare earth magnets; there is currently no easy substitute.

**[USA Rare Earth (USAR)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/USAR)** — Nasdaq, USA | Rare Earths & Magnets | **Moonshot**

Recently went public and has started producing rare earth metals and permanent magnets at its Texas facility. Reported $6M in Q1 2026 revenue — real but small. The US military wants domestic magnet supply and has begun awarding contracts to companies building it. Higher risk than MP Materials but more upside if government procurement ramps.

### Robotics & Automation

**[Cognex (CGNX)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/CGNX)** — Nasdaq, USA | Machine Vision | **Established**

Makes the "eyes" of factories and warehouses — machine vision cameras and software that guide robots, inspect products for defects, and read barcodes on a production line. If robot deployments accelerate (as widely expected with AI-driven automation), every robot needs vision systems like Cognex's. Pivoting toward a software subscription model, which typically attracts higher stock valuations over time.

**[Keyence (KYCCF)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/KYCCF)** — OTC (ADR), Japan | Industrial Sensors & Machine Vision | **Established**

Japan's most profitable industrial company by margin — makes sensors, machine vision systems, and measuring instruments that factories worldwide depend on. Trades in the US as an ADR (American Depositary Receipt — a US-listed share representing stock in a foreign company, letting US investors buy it like any US stock without opening a foreign brokerage account). Often overlooked by US-focused investors despite being a dominant global franchise.

As geopolitical tensions rise, governments want more real-time imagery of adversary activity — Planet's daily-revisit constellation fills that gap. Still unprofitable but a rare pure-play on satellite data as a service.

### Biotech / Lab Tooling

As GLP-1 obesity drugs, gene therapies, and biosimilars (lower-cost versions of biologic drugs) all ramp production, the factories that make them need more of Repligen's single-use equipment. Reported Q1 2026 sales of €748M, up 8% at constant exchange rates (meaning growth after stripping out currency swings between euros and dollars). Strong recovery after biotech companies worked through excess inventory in 2023–24.

## Section 3: Sector Catalysts

*Major contracts, capex announcements (capex = capital expenditures, meaning money spent on long-lived assets like buildings, equipment, and infrastructure), or policy shifts that lift a whole sector at once.*

**Hyperscaler AI capex blitz: ~$690B in 2026**
Amazon (~$200B), Alphabet/Google (~$180B), Microsoft (~$145B annualized), and Meta (~$125B) are collectively pouring roughly $690 billion into AI infrastructure this year — equal to roughly the GDP of Switzerland, all directed at chips, data centers, power systems, and cooling. About 75% flows into physical hardware and infrastructure. This is the single largest capital expenditure cycle in the history of technology, and every upstream supplier benefits.

**Golden Dome missile defense: $13.4B appropriated in FY2026, potential $175B–$1.2T over decades**
Congress approved $13.4 billion in fiscal 2026 for the Golden Dome program — the Pentagon's new layered missile defense system that uses satellites, sensors in space, and interceptors to protect the US from ballistic and hypersonic missiles. The Congressional Budget Office estimates the full program could cost $1.2 trillion over 20 years. Defense electronics makers, space sensor suppliers, nuclear propulsion companies, and precision motion-control specialists are early beneficiaries — the quiet upstream names, not just the headline prime contractors.

**China's rare earth export crackdown: Western supply chain urgency elevated**
China controls roughly 90% of global rare earth refining and has escalated export restrictions on rare earth elements in 2026 in response to US trade policy. Rare earths are used in permanent magnets inside EV motors, wind turbines, hard drives, guided missiles, and some semiconductor manufacturing equipment. The crackdown has created acute urgency for the US and allied nations to build alternative supply chains — a direct, government-accelerated tailwind for Western rare earth miners and processors such as [MP Materials (MP)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/MP) and [USA Rare Earth (USAR)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/USAR).

**DOE's $900M SMR deployment funding unlocked**
The US Department of Energy is offering $900 million to support the first commercial SMR deployments in the US — $800M for up to two "first mover" utility teams and $100M for additional projects. This significantly de-risks early SMR investments. First-movers like [Oklo (OKLO)](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/OKLO) and X-Energy (pre-IPO) are best positioned to capture this money, given their advanced development timelines.

**EU's €200B "AI Continent" Action Plan**
The European Union has launched a €200 billion investment plan for AI infrastructure — €50B in public funds and €150B from private sources — aimed at semiconductor factories and AI data centers on European soil. This creates additional demand for semiconductor equipment, power infrastructure, and cooling hardware, benefiting suppliers globally including US-listed names that sell into Europe.
