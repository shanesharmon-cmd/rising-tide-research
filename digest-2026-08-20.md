## 3. Sector Catalysts

- **Hyperscaler spending stays enormous.** Microsoft, Alphabet (Google), Amazon, Meta, and Oracle have collectively committed to roughly $660–690 billion in capex (capex = capital expenditure, meaning money spent on big physical investments like buildings and equipment) for 2026 — nearly double 2025 — with about 75% of it going toward AI infrastructure. That spending flows down to chipmaking-equipment, cooling, and power suppliers.
- **The grid equipment shortage is a multi-year story.** Power transformers now have lead times of four-plus years, largely because of a bottleneck in GOES (the specialty electrical steel transformers require) — the US has only one domestic maker of it. The Department of Energy put $375 million toward fixing the domestic supply chain, and Hitachi Energy is investing $1 billion in a new transformer factory in Virginia. More than half of planned 2026 US data centers are reportedly at risk of delay because of this shortage — a tailwind for steelmakers and equipment builders who can help solve it.
- **The Pentagon is racing to secure rare earths and drones — at the same time.** The Department of Defense wants over 300,000 drones by 2028, but the magnets in those drones depend almost entirely on rare earth materials, 98% of which come from China. The Pentagon has signed over $2 billion in deals to secure critical minerals and batteries domestically, and new rules will effectively ban Chinese-origin rare earths from the US defense supply chain starting in 2027 — a direct tailwind for US rare-earth and magnet suppliers.
- **Big industrials are paying up for cooling technology.** Ecolab's acquisition of liquid-cooling company CoolIT Systems closed at a reported 29 times next-twelve-months EBITDA (earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization — a common way to value a company's core profitability) — a rich price that signals how much value large industrial players now place on data-center cooling technology. Separately, Johnson Controls agreed to acquire thermal-management company Alloy Enterprises for the same reason.
