1. NEW CANDIDATES

Note: the names below come from my general knowledge of these sectors. Today's searches did not confirm their latest numbers.

  • Camtek (CAMT) — Nasdaq (Israel-based). Chip-making equipment. It makes machines that inspect finished chips for defects. More advanced AI chips mean more inspection. Established.
  • Onto Innovation (ONTO) — Nasdaq (US). Chip-making equipment. It sells tools that measure and inspect chips while they are built. Chip giants need these as designs get more complex. Established.
  • BWX Technologies (BWXT) — NYSE (US). Nuclear and defense. It builds nuclear reactor components for the US Navy and works on new reactor fuel and parts. More reactors, including small ones, mean more demand. Established.
  • Amentum (AMTM) — NYSE (US). Engineering services. It leads a group that won a $406 million contract to be the "owner's engineer" (the expert who checks the work for the buyer) on the UK's small nuclear reactor program. Established.
  • Fluor (FLR) — NYSE (US). Construction. It signed a contract to support X-energy (XE)'s planned small reactor project in Texas. Established.
  • Lynas Rare Earths (LYC.AX) — ASX (Australia). Rare earths (metals used in magnets for chips, motors and weapons). It is a non-Chinese supplier, and a US rule from late July 2026 pushes defense makers away from Chinese supply. Established.
  • USA Rare Earth (USAR) — Nasdaq (US). Rare earth magnets and materials. China put it on an export control list in June 2026, so it is a direct target in the supply fight. It is likely not yet consistently profitable; check. Moonshot.

3. SECTOR CATALYSTS

  • Big tech spending (capex = money spent on buildings and equipment). TrendForce estimates the top nine cloud companies will spend about $830 billion in 2026, up 79%. Reported plans: Microsoft (MSFT) about $190 billion, Alphabet (GOOGL) $180–190 billion, Meta Platforms (META) $125–145 billion. Suppliers get the orders.
  • Power and cooling. Vertiv (VRT) reportedly raised 2026 revenue guidance to $13.5–14 billion and has a $15 billion backlog (orders not yet delivered). Power supply is the main limit on new data centers.
  • Chip equipment. Commentary says chip toolmakers like ASML (ASML), Applied Materials (AMAT) and Lam Research (LRCX) have been beating Nvidia this year. These are well known and may be pricey.
  • Rare earths deadline. One analysis says China's suspension of its export limits lasts until November 10, 2026. If it ends, prices for non-Chinese suppliers like MP Materials (MP) could jump. China also put MP Materials on its export control list in June.
  • Small nuclear. The UK signed a contract with Rolls-Royce (RR.L) for three small reactors at Wylfa, Wales. AtkinsRéalis (ATRL.TO) got a $450 million contract for the first Darlington (Canada) small reactor unit; the date was not given.
